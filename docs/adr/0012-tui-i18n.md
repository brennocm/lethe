# 0012. TUI and operator texts

- Status: accepted
- Date: 2026-11-16

## Context
M12 delivers the TUI (spec §16.5): the same information as the GUI, the keys `p`/`a`/`s`/`q`,
and, under `run --tui` without a GUI, the full-screen alert with a red screen and the terminal
bell. The alert screen needs, per check, what the finding means and what to do now (§16.3);
§16.6 puts these texts in `internal/i18n/*.toml`, with a test that fails when a key is missing.
The spec did not say how the TUI reaches the daemon inside `run`, how goldens are taken, or what
`q` and Ctrl-C do while an alert is on screen.

## Decision
- **The TUI is an IPC client, even inside `run --tui`** (`tui.IPC`). It goes through the same
  peer check, passphrase proof (`auth.challenge` + HMAC) and failure budget (SELF-006) as
  `lethe ack`, so no in-process path skips the checks. The model sees only `tui.Backend`. The
  daemon's output is written to a ring buffer (`tui.Lines`) that the TUI shows, so it does not
  draw over the screen. If the arm fails, the last lines go to stderr.
- **Alert screen:** it shows the oldest open, unacknowledged CRITICAL alert, with every line on a
  red background. It contains:
  - the title, the meaning and what to do;
  - destination and process, from the clear data of the alert's `check.alert`; sealed fields
    never reach IPC;
  - the actions already taken (`response.executed` for the alert, failures marked);
  - the lockdown and how to get out.

  The bell (`\a`) rings at once and then every `ui.sound_repeat`. Esc and `q` do not close the
  screen; only `a` does: reason, then passphrase, for a CRITICAL. A WARNING needs only a reason,
  as `alert.ack` requires. Ctrl-C leaves the TUI and makes `run` disarm, as the signal does
  without a TUI. `q` (outside an alert) leaves the TUI and keeps the session armed until the
  signal.
- **Selftest from the TUI:** `s` offers a complete run (RESP-001 included, passphrase, the only
  run that updates `selftest.json`) or a quick one (every case but RESP-001, no passphrase).
- **The session fingerprint goes in the header**, because the alt screen hides the line `run`
  prints before it.
- **The passphrase being typed** is kept in a fixed 256-byte, mlocked buffer, written with
  `utf8.EncodeRune` and never converted to a string. It is wiped after the key is derived, and on
  Esc or quit.
- **Texts:** `internal/i18n/{pt-BR,en-US}.toml` hold `[ui]`, `[actions]` and one section per
  check with `title`, `meaning` and `todo`. The format is a TOML subset (comments, `[section]`,
  `key = "text"` with `\"`, `\\` and `\n`), read by a 100-line parser instead of a TOML library
  outside §20.1. The files are embedded and come from the repository, never from users; the
  parser has a fuzz target. `checks.Title` now reads these files, so the agent's notifications
  and the TUI share one source. Tests check that both languages have the same keys, that nothing
  is empty, and that every registered check has the three texts.
- **Colour** comes from hand-written SGR codes; lipgloss is only a transitive dependency of
  bubbletea and is not used directly.
- **Tests:** `github.com/charmbracelet/x/exp/teatest` and `.../x/exp/golden` are test-only
  dependencies outside §20.1. They are the libraries the spec names for M12 (§24), MIT
  licensed, and from the bubbletea authors. The goldens compare `View()` at fixed states (main
  screen and alert screen), with a fixed clock and UTC. They do not compare the raw terminal
  stream, whose cursor moves depend on the renderer. The interaction tests use teatest with a
  fake backend; `TestE2E_M12_TUIOverIPC` runs the TUI against an armed daemon in the netlab.

## Consequences
- The seccomp allowlist (M14, phase 2) must allow the terminal syscalls bubbletea makes after
  arm (`ioctl` TCGETS/TCSETS/TIOCGWINSZ, SIGWINCH).
- A new check needs its section in both TOML files, or the checks test fails.
